The starting point

Technology is most useful when it makes a difficult thing clearer, safer, easier to understand, or easier to act on.

01
Illya Knight / Founder & Managing Member

Start with the problem.

For Illya Knight, the useful question is rarely “What technology can we build?” It is closer to: what are people repeatedly struggling to see, connect, understand, or manage — and is there a responsible way to make that work easier?

That orientation shapes Function Media's work: observe the real environment first, then determine whether technology has a meaningful role in improving it.

02
Shea Johnson / Co-Owner

Keep the human outcome visible.

Shea Johnson brings the same work back to the people around the system: the staff who have to use it, the communities affected by it, and the organizations responsible for making decisions with it.

The point is not complexity for its own sake. The point is to build deliberately enough that sophisticated systems can still support understandable, accountable human work.

“An idea becomes valuable when it can leave the room, meet the real world, and make something work better.”
Function Media LLC / Operating philosophy
01 / From observation to infrastructure

Ideas don't begin as products.

They can begin as a recurring frustration: information that exists but is difficult to connect; a process that asks people to reconstruct context manually; a decision that depends on evidence scattered across different systems.

Function Media approaches those moments as design questions. Before naming a product or choosing a technical architecture, the company asks what the actual problem is, who experiences it, what information already exists, and what a more useful outcome would look like.

02 / The common thread

Different domains. A familiar problem.

A food-safety investigation, a district-operations question, and a mission-oriented intelligence problem can appear unrelated. But each can involve the same deeper difficulty: important context distributed across people, records, systems, and time.

Function Media's public work explores how better organization of authorized information can help people recognize relationships while preserving provenance, boundaries, and human review.

04 / Ease is not the absence of sophistication

Good systems can carry complexity without handing all of it to the user.

“With ease” does not mean pretending hard problems are simple. It means doing the difficult design work so that the person responsible for acting can spend more time understanding the situation and less time fighting the machinery around it.

That is an important distinction for Function Media. The company is interested in sophisticated infrastructure, but sophistication is not the outcome. The outcome is better-supported human work.
